Types of Gutter Guards We Install

We offer several gutter guard options. Micro-mesh gutter guards use fine stainless steel mesh that blocks even small debris while allowing maximum water flow. Reverse curve (surface tension) guards use the principle of water adhesion to direct water into the gutter while debris falls off the edge. Foam inserts fit inside the gutter and allow water to pass through while blocking debris. Brush guards use bristles to catch debris while allowing water through. We recommend the best type based on your roof type, surrounding trees, and budget.

Benefits of Gutter Guards

Gutter guards offer numerous benefits. They eliminate or dramatically reduce the need for gutter cleaning — most homes with gutter guards need cleaning only every 3-5 years instead of twice a year. They prevent clogs that cause water overflow and damage to your roof, siding, and foundation. They keep pests like birds, squirrels, and insects from nesting in your gutters. They prevent ice dams by ensuring water can flow freely even in winter. And they provide peace of mind knowing your gutters are working properly year-round.

How Gutter Guards Work

Gutter guards work by creating a barrier that allows water to enter your gutters while keeping debris out. Different types use different mechanisms. Micro-mesh gutter guards use fine stainless steel mesh with openings small enough to block even pine needles and roof grit while allowing water to pass through. The mesh is typically installed at an angle so debris slides off. Reverse curve (surface tension) guards use the principle of water adhesion — water flows over a curved surface and into the gutter while debris falls off the edge. Foam inserts fit inside the gutter and allow water to pass through the foam while blocking larger debris. Brush guards use bristles that catch debris while allowing water to flow through. Each type has advantages, and we recommend the best option based on your specific needs.

Gutter Guard Maintenance

While gutter guards dramatically reduce the need for cleaning, they don't eliminate it entirely. Over time, small debris like pollen, roof grit, and seeds can accumulate on top of the guards. In some cases, debris can build up on top of the guards and prevent water from entering the gutters. We recommend annual inspection of your gutter guards to ensure they're functioning properly. If debris has accumulated on top, we can clean it off quickly. Most homes with gutter guards need professional cleaning every 3-5 years instead of twice a year. We offer maintenance plans that include annual inspection and cleaning as needed.
